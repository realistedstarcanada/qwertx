02 / Market explainers

This week’s smart money angle

Week ending 16 October 2026.

CRWV16 Oct 2026

CoreWeave slides on AI-debt worries

What happenedCoreWeave fell about 12% on Friday, its second big drop in three days, as investors focused on the AI cloud provider’s roughly $50 billion of debt.

What it could meanCompanies that borrow to build AI data centres now trade like rate-sensitive stocks. With 10-year yields above 5%, financing costs matter as much as demand. Expect volatility until earnings show cash flow catching up with spending.

1 super investor own it: David Tepper (1.4%)
David Tepper’s Appaloosa opened a new position in Q2 2026, before this sell-off.
META16 Oct 2026

Meta falls 4% ahead of earnings

What happenedMeta dropped nearly 4% on Friday as the AI sell-off hit the biggest spenders on AI infrastructure.

What it could meanUpcoming Big Tech earnings must show AI investment turning into revenue and cash flow. Meta’s ad business funds its spending, which separates it from borrowers, but the market wants proof.

6 super investors own it: Bill Ackman (9.2%), Chase Coleman (6.6%), David Tepper (4.9%), Terry Smith (4.3%) and 2 more
One of the most widely held stocks among our super investors.
NFLX16 Oct 2026

Netflix reports earnings on 20 October

What happenedNetflix reports third-quarter results on Tuesday, 20 October, at the start of a heavy week of earnings.

What it could meanSubscriber growth, ad-tier revenue and margins will set the tone. A streaming business is less exposed to AI financing fears, which may help it stand out in a jittery tape.

4 super investors own it: Bill Ackman (4.8%), Terry Smith (3.7%), Bill Nygren (1.0%), Thomas Gayner (0.0%)
Bill Ackman’s Pershing Square opened a new position worth about $934 million in Q2 2026.
OXY16 Oct 2026

Oil above $100 lifts energy stocks

What happenedEnergy was the best sector on Friday (up about 2.5%) as WTI crude climbed above $101 a barrel on Middle East supply risks.

What it could meanProducers like Occidental earn much more cash at these prices, which supports buybacks and debt paydown. The risk is that oil prices fall back quickly if supply eases.

2 super investors own it: Warren Buffett (4.3%), Duan Yongping (2.6%)
Berkshire Hathaway owns about 265 million Occidental shares, one of its largest positions.
