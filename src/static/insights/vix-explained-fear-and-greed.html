VIX explained: reading market fear and greed

What the VIX measures, what its levels mean and how to use it without overreacting.

What is the VIX?

The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) measures how much movement options traders expect from the S&P 500 over the next 30 days. When investors rush to buy protection, option prices rise and the VIX goes up.

That is why it is often called the market’s “fear gauge”.

Reading the levels

Below 12: extreme calm, sometimes complacency. 12 to 15: calm. 15 to 20: normal. 20 to 30: elevated fear. Above 30: panic, usually during sharp sell-offs.

These bands are a rule of thumb, not a law, but they give useful context for daily headlines.

How long-term investors use it

Spikes in the VIX have often coincided with good long-term buying opportunities, because prices fall fastest when fear peaks. Very low readings can be a reminder to stay disciplined rather than chase.

The VIX doesn’t predict direction. Use it as context for your own plan, not as a trading signal.
